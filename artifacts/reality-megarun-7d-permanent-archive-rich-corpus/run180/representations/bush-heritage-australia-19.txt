One of Edgbaston’s highest priorities is protecting
the critically endangered Red-finned Blue-eye, a tiny
fish found only in these springs. After nearly a decade
of experience breeding the species in captivity, we
continue to build the species' resilience against future
threats. This year, we built a new breeding facility and an
emergency holding facility for the fish.
The team is also embracing cutting-edge technology
to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of our
conservation management. Environmental DNA sampling
allows scientists to detect species and assess genetic
diversity by analysing traces of skin cells and mucus in
water, reducing the need to capture animals. Drones
carrying LiDAR and other remote sensing hardware
provide centimetre-scale mapping of wetlands, helping to
target weeds, track landscape change, map the impacts
of feral pigs and predict the spread of introduced fish,
Gambusia.
