# Scale-to-100 estimate

## Measured basis

The only proposition-count basis suitable for extrapolation is the five-subject Spike-8 replay: **101 propositions / 5 subjects = 20.2 per subject** (range 6–36). The report stress provides an independent density signal of **16 accepted candidates / 2 rich subjects = 8**, but is deliberately a partial report slice. G2 review measurement is **27 units / 23 propositions = 1.17 units/proposition**, including four relation decisions. Spike 13 confirms that changing the card architecture left review at 16 units.

## Estimate

| Scenario | Proposition semantics | 100-subject count | Review implication |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Sparse regulator-shaped | 6 / subject | 600 | roughly 700 G2 review units at 1.17x, before policy automation. |
| Retained-cohort average | 20.2 / subject | 2,020 | roughly 2,370 review units; wide variance dominates planning. |
| Rich retained baseline | 33–36 / subject | 3,300–3,600 | roughly 3,870–4,230 units plus complex-domain escalation. |
| Partial rich-report bridge | 8 / subject | 800 | not a coverage forecast; only a lower-density partial-report path. |

These are **reviewable proposition semantics**, not 2,000 card fields, model calls, or human approvals. Duplicate evidence can support an existing proposition without becoming a new review unit; coverage is separately counted. A sensible operating assumption is therefore a 600–3,600 proposition envelope for 100 subjects, with 2,020 as the retained-cohort planning midpoint—not an intuition-derived “20 sections × 100” calculation.

## Review policy implication

Human review should be allocated by risk and novelty: high-risk conduct/outcome/relationship and domain-extension cases require review; low-risk structured regulator facts can move under an explicit automation policy after calibration. The campaign does not support automatic conflict resolution: it saw zero substantive retained conflicts. It does support reviewing relations separately, because 4/23 G2 fixture units were relations and scope/time near-collisions were correctly kept out of the conflict bucket.

The largest scale variable is report availability and proposition density, not whether the user sees a progressive card or a 20-section dossier. Card compilation changes translations; it did not change the measured semantic review count.
