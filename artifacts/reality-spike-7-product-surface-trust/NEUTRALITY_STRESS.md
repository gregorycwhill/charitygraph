# Neutrality stress

Attribution establishes who said something and where; it does not establish that it is true, complete, comparable, causally valid, or neutral.

|Claim type|Is P1 attribution sufficient?|Why / higher gate|
|---|---|---|
|Descriptive programme, form, named report structure|Sometimes, with exact locator and first-party label|Low inference, but retain “reported” wording and no comparative framing.| 
|Promotional ROI or uplift|No|A charity’s claimed uplift is method-dependent and promotional. Require methodology, scope/period, independent or adjudicated evidence, and governed comparative language.| 
|Outcome claim|No for conclusion; limited P1 for “the report says it measured/reported…”|Outcomes invite causal or effectiveness inference. Require outcome-method review and evidence-strength display before evaluative summarisation.| 
|Conduct/adverse claim|No|Omission and wording are legally/reputationally sensitive. Require authoritative source, corroboration/response handling, and hard governance.| 
|Governance assertion|Usually no|A report can name roles or attest controls, but assertions about quality, independence, compliance, or adequacy require contextual review. Simple office-holder extraction remains P1-capable with date/locator.| 
|Relationship assertion|No where ownership, dependence, endorsement, or conflict is implied|Entity resolution and relationship type can materially mislead. Require a controlled relation schema and human promotion.| 

The prototypes therefore label “ACFID Code commitment”, “transparency/accountability/integrity”, audit statements, and outcome-report coverage as first-party report claims—not CharityGraph endorsements. Do not convert absence of a conduct item into a clean-risk signal. P1’s caution surface says “no retained adverse-evidence processing here”, never “no adverse conduct”.
